**Color accessibility \- WCAG 2.1 Guidelines**

A great way to make sure your designs are accessible is to check the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 ([https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/)) contrast ratio for all text elements in components. At the most basic level WCAG 2.1 guidelines are categorized into three different levels based on how accessible they are.

As a good general rule, maintain at least a 4:5:1 contrast ratio (WCAG AA) for all of your most crucial UI elements (text, buttons, forms etc.). Meeting the AA requirement is sufficient for the majority of users and compensates for the loss of contrast sensitivity by users with a 20/40 vision loss.

There are some handy Figma plugins to check contrast ratios right in Figma as you design. Our favorite is the Stark which allows you to check the color contrast ratio of all visible text in a frame.

Neutral 400: 1.55:1  
**Level A \- low contrast-less than 4.5:1**

Not suitable any text elements. You can ignore the contrast on decorations and non-important parts.

Neutral 600: 4.83:1  
**Level AA \- med contrast-more than 4.5:1**

A minimum level of accessibility for all your most crucial UI elements (text, buttons, forms etc.).

Neutral 1000: 17.73:1  
**Level AAA-high contrast — more than 7:1**

Super-high contrast isn't a requirement, but it's a nice-to- have strive for it where possible\!
